There are many Old Testament prophecies fulfilled by Jesus Christ. Most of them can
be seen in papers such as [this one published in Liberty University's *Second Person*.](https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1154&context=second_person)

But there are also many other subtle, yet powerful, fulfillments in Jesus' life,
told through parallels.

In fact, the entire Old Testament is a shadow of Christ and His glory, pointing to Him
through various figures. Likewise, the New Testament mirrors and retells the Old Testament, now illuminated by the light that was missing: our Lord Jesus Christ Himself.

One such occasion is the period of 40 days and nights that Christ spent in the wilderness.

## 40 Days in the Wilderness

{{< figure src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/Christ_in_the_Wilderness_-_Ivan_Kramskoy_-_Google_Cultural_Institute.jpg" alt="Christ in the Wilderness by Ivan Kramskoy" width="500" caption="Christ in the Wilderness by Ivan Kramskoy" align="center" >}}

Matthew 4 describes Jesus being led by the Holy Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted.
Here, the number 40 is no coincidence, nor is it merely a surface-level parallel to the 40 years
the Hebrew people spent in the wilderness after God led them out of captivity in Egypt.

Rather, it is retelling a story that was about Jesus Himself all along.

Let's take a look at the three temptations Jesus faced there:

### 1. The Needs of the Flesh

First, Satan tempts Jesus to turn stones into bread. As the Son of God, He easily could have.

Jesus answers by quoting Deuteronomy 8:3, a passage about Israel learning to depend on God
for food in the wilderness, rather than on their own strength.

{{< bible ref="Deuteronomy 8:3" version="KJV" >}}
And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the Lord doth man live.
{{< /bible >}}

### 2. Pride and Testing God

Next, Satan tempts Jesus to jump from the highest point in Jerusalem, suggesting the angels
would save Him. As the Son of God, He would be protected.

Again, Jesus quotes Deuteronomy, commanding not to put the Lord to the test.

{{< bible ref="Deuteronomy 6:16" version="KJV" >}}
Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah
{{< /bible >}}

### 3. Power, Glory, and Worship

Finally, Satan tempts Jesus to trade His eternal glory for the glory of this world.

Once again, Jesus quotes from Deuteronomy, declaring that we must worship the Lord alone.

{{< bible ref="Deuteronomy 6:13" version="KJV" >}}
Thou shalt fear the LORD thy God, and serve him, and shalt swear by his name.
{{< /bible >}}

## Israel Failed, Jesus Succeeded

All three temptations and answers draw from the same book, which
records Israel's failures in the wilderness, now contrasted with the triumph of Christ.

There's so much that can be extract from this passage in terms of spiritual knowledge
and teachings, but let's just highlight this: 

**Jesus faced the exact same tests and succeeded, while Israel fell short.**
